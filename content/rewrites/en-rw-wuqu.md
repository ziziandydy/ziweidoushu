Wu Qu (武曲) is a Northern Dipper star of yin metal in Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Chinese system of Purple Star Astrology, and classical texts name it both the "wealth star" and the "general star." Put the two labels side by side and the portrait is more interesting than "good with money." Wu Qu governs execution. People with this star prominent in their charts rarely enjoy abstract talk. They tend to ask, "So what do we do now?" Money is one of the results of that habit, not the whole of it.

## Earning through discipline, not inspiration

Tan Lang (貪狼) is also a wealth star, but it relies on contacts and chance. Wu Qu relies on routine and professional skill. The typical path is clear: do one thing reliably, then let accumulation turn it into income, rather than bet everything on a single lucky move. Wu Qu people are sensitive to numbers. They work out the margin when they see a quote, and when they hear an investment pitch, their first question is where the risk lies.

The same traits have costs. Wu Qu follows plans closely and can look stiff when reality departs from them. It is also less sensitive to other people's feelings, speaks directly and avoids detours, so colleagues sometimes find such a person hard to approach. Usually this is not indifference. It is a lack of practice in saying hard things softly.

Consider an invented example, a procurement lead called Sam. He is an illustration and not a real person or a real chart. Sam's supplier misses a deadline and he calls a meeting of five minutes: three alternatives, one decision, a clear deadline. The team respects how fast it was done and privately wishes he had asked how they felt about the overtime. A single added sentence, "I know this lands on your weekend, and here is how we will make up for it," would cost him thirty seconds and change how the decision is received.

## The company Wu Qu keeps

Wu Qu shares a palace with another major star in five combinations, and only one position is solitary. With Tian Fu (天府) in the Zi and Wu positions, the earner and the vault work together, producing steady income and spending with restraint. With Tian Xiang (天相) in Yin and Shen, financial and administrative skills are good and promises are kept. With Qi Sha (七殺) in Mao and You, drive is strong and decisions are fast, and the person is good at earning but can seem distant in love. With Tan Lang in Chou and Wei, an old saying describes "poor first, then rich": opportunities arrive slowly in youth and ripen in middle age, so this is a pairing for the long run. With Po Jun (破軍) in Si and Hai, change is largest, since earning and spending are both large, and the person needs deliberate financial discipline. Alone in the Chen and Xu positions, Wu Qu is the purest and often the loneliest version, handling everything personally.

## All four transformations, each with a clear meaning

Wu Qu receives every annual transformation. A Ji year gives Hua Lu (化祿), a Geng year gives Hua Quan (化權), a Jia year gives Hua Ke (化科), and a Ren year gives Hua Ji (化忌).

Ji-year Hua Lu is earned income: salary, business profit and the return on the main job, more than windfalls. Geng-year Hua Quan means the chance to decide about money rises, whether as a finance head, a manager or an owner. Jia-year Hua Ke points to professional reputation, especially in areas that depend on trust, such as finance, accounting, law and technical work.

The Ren-year version is the one to handle with care. The Zhongzhou school, which this site follows, reads Hua Ji as fixation. For Wu Qu that means being unable to let go in money matters: clinging to what is held and missing what is new, or doubling down to recover a loss. People born in a Ren year, or those meeting Wu Qu with Hua Ji in an annual cycle, should ask a second person before major investments and loans, rather than calculating alone. The overall logic of these influences is set out in [Four Transformations in Zi Wei Dou Shu: How Life Forces Shift](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/four-transformations-in-zi-wei-dou-shu-how-life-forces-shift).

Wu Qu in a Wealth Palace (財帛宮) does not guarantee a full purse. The ability to earn is stronger than average, but whether money stays depends on neighbors. If Po Jun, Qing Yang (擎羊), Di Kong (地空) or Di Jie (地劫) join the palace or its supporting palaces, there are many ways in and many ways out. Tian Fu, Lu Cun (祿存) or Hua Lu nearby help it stay. The gap between earning and holding is explored in [Made More Money But Can't Keep It? Zi Wei Dou Shu on Earning vs. Holding Wealth](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/made-more-money-but-cant-keep-it-zi-wei-dou-shu-earning-vs-holding-wealth).

In a Career Palace (官祿宮), Wu Qu performs best in places with structure: finance, engineering, operations and law, anywhere a messy project must be broken into a list with deadlines. It is less comfortable when the task is to win hearts. A habit that helps is adding one slide to every proposal that answers, "What does this give the other side?" It is usually more persuasive than ten pages of data.

In a Spouse Palace (夫妻宮), love is practical as well. Wu Qu people express care through actions: remembering expenses, arranging travel, planning ahead. A partner who hopes for emotional language may feel little warmth. After doing the practical thing, add one short sentence, "I did this because I care about you." It is brief and often what the other person was waiting to hear.
