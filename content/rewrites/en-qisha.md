Qi Sha (七殺) is a Southern Dipper star of yin metal with a streak of fire, and Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Chinese system of Purple Star Astrology, calls it the "general star." A general commands troops and decides under pressure, and the word "sha" means to cut cleanly without leaving loose ends. The picture is clear: someone with strong drive who does not like to drag things out and moves once the direction is set. Many people assume this means a harsh temper. The more common difficulty is solitude. A person who is used to carrying things alone seldom lets anyone see how heavy they are.

An invented example helps. Take a project manager at an engineering firm, whom we will call Ray. He is not a real person and this is not a real chart; he is a figure made up to illustrate the star. A key project hits trouble a week before delivery: a supplier is late, and the client chooses this moment to change the specification. The team offers opinions in every direction. Some suggest a delay, some suggest adding staff, and one person wants a meeting first. Ray listens for ten minutes and cuts in: "Tonight we find replacement suppliers. Spec changes are accepted only if they cost us under three days. I want the new schedule on my desk at nine tomorrow."

## Command without a crowd

That is Qi Sha at work. Ray listened and heard enough. When the information is adequate he makes the call at once, and the more chaotic the scene, the calmer he gets. This suits project management, entrepreneurship, military or police service, surgery, sport and business development, any job where someone has to decide fast and own the result.

After the project ships, the team goes to dinner and Ray goes home early to cook noodles. He is not unhappy. There is a faint distance, because everyone knows he worked hard but nobody knows what the decision cost him in pressure. This is the side of Qi Sha people seldom mention: independence breeds loneliness. A healthy habit is a regular outlet, such as a weekly conversation with someone trustworthy that does not need to solve anything.

## Who sits beside Qi Sha

The star changes hardness depending on its companion. With Zi Wei (紫微) in the Si and Hai positions, authority and drive combine, decisions come quickly and teams follow, though the temper can run direct and heavy-handed. With Wu Qu (武曲), two metal stars meet in the Mao and You positions: stiff, practical, strong at earning money, and often cool in romance. With Lian Zhen (廉貞) in the Chou and Wei positions, loves and hatreds are sharp and effort is intense, with a tendency toward extremes. Qi Sha alone in the Zi and Wu positions is called "Qi Sha facing the Dipper" in classical texts and counts as a place where it works well, with obvious authority and skill. In its other solitary positions it keeps its drive but needs to avoid doing everything personally.

## No transformations, and a pattern of upheaval

Qi Sha is one of the few major stars that none of the ten heavenly stems transforms. It never becomes Hua Lu (化祿), Hua Quan (化權), Hua Ke (化科) or Hua Ji (化忌) by birth year. Its character is therefore more stable than most, and annual influences rarely act on it directly. The price is that nothing triggers a turning point from outside; a Qi Sha person who wants change usually has to start it. For how the four transformations work elsewhere, see [Four Transformations in Zi Wei Dou Shu: How Life Forces Shift](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/four-transformations-in-zi-wei-dou-shu-how-life-forces-shift).

Qi Sha belongs to the pattern known as Sha Po Lang, together with Po Jun (破軍) and Tan Lang (貪狼). The Zhongzhou school treats it as a pioneering pattern: more change, more ups and downs, and more chance to build something independently. People with it in the Life Palace (命宮) or its supporting palaces seldom thrive in environments that reward only stability. Start-ups, sales, technical development and freelance work suit them better. The sibling star in the pattern is described in [Detailed Explanation of the Po Jun Star: Life Turning Points of Destruction and Rebirth](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/detailed-explanation-of-the-po-jun-star-life-turning-points-of-destruction-and-r).

In the Spouse Palace (夫妻宮), Qi Sha brings directness and a strong sense of commitment. Someone they like is pursued openly, and someone they do not like is not kept waiting. The usual blind spot is handling a relationship the way a project is handled: a problem appears, so it must be solved immediately, even when the other person only wanted to be heard. A workable adjustment is "listen for five minutes before offering a plan." Those five minutes may be the hardest part for a Qi Sha person, and they are often the most useful.

Money habits follow the same temperament. When Qi Sha sits in the Wealth Palace (財帛宮), income tends to come from bold moves such as launching, closing deals or taking a risk others avoided, and cash flow can swing with them. A simple safeguard is to keep six months of expenses untouched, so that a bold move never forces a hasty sale.

Ray later made two rules. Before any major decision he writes down three options and then chooses one, so that instinct does not decide alone. After each project he asks the team one question: "What did you most need support with this time?" After three months people were bringing problems to him earlier, and he no longer carried every burden by himself. The same applies at work: Ray sets a ten-minute pause before he announces any decision that affects other people's weekends, long enough to ask who will carry the cost and whether they have been told. Qi Sha's strength is decisiveness, and it earns more followers when the doubts that came before the decision are shared too.
