The twelve palaces are the skeleton of a Zi Wei Dou Shu chart, the Chinese system of Purple Star Astrology, and the most common beginner mistake is to read them one at a time. A frightening star in the Wealth Palace gets all the attention while a calm star in the opposite palace is ignored. The Zhongzhou school, which this site follows, reads palaces in pairs and in groups. The easiest way to show what that means is a conversation, so here is one. Both speakers are invented for this explanation and are not real people.

## "Should I change jobs?"

Ahmed, a mid-career analyst, sits down with a more experienced friend, Lena, and says, "My Career Palace looks ordinary. Is that a reason not to move?" Lena does not look at the Career Palace first. She points to the Life Palace (命宮) and the palace opposite it, the Travel Palace (遷移宮). "The Life Palace is your own base color. The Travel Palace is the outside world and how others see you. If the Travel Palace is stronger, a change of setting usually helps. If it carries harsh stars while the Life Palace is steady, the outside is harder, and it is better to firm up your base first."

Ahmed's Travel Palace holds supportive stars. "Then I can move?" "Not yet. The Career Palace (官祿宮) tells you what type of work suits you. Yours leans toward building something new, and the start-up you are considering fits that. Now look at the Parents Palace (父母宮). In our school it also stands for bosses and people above you. Is it the manager you cannot stand, or the entire field?" Ahmed admits it is the manager. Lena nods. "Then a different team may be enough."

## "I earn well, so why do I feel insecure?"

Ahmed's next question concerns money. Lena turns to the Wealth Palace (財帛宮) and the one opposite it. "The Wealth Palace shows how money arrives and what you believe about it, not how much there is. Its opposite is the Fortune Palace (福德宮), which governs inner calm. Read them together. Your Wealth Palace has Hua Lu, so income is steady, but the Fortune Palace has harsh stars, which is why you have money and no peace." She points to the Property Palace (田宅宮). "That is the vault. Outside assets sit there, and inner security sits in the Fortune Palace. You need both for what the school calls a settled life."

Ahmed remembers checking his savings at midnight last month. Lena suggests splitting the worry into three separate questions: is the income stable, is the cushion large enough, and is the unease about money or about something else? Each has a different remedy, and only the first two are solved with numbers. The difference between earning and keeping is examined in [Made More Money But Can't Keep It? Zi Wei Dou Shu on Earning vs. Holding Wealth](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/made-more-money-but-cant-keep-it-zi-wei-dou-shu-earning-vs-holding-wealth).

## Relationships, health, and the body palace

"What about my partner and me? We argue about the same thing every time." Lena looks at the Spouse Palace (夫妻宮). "It shows your habits inside a relationship, but compare it with the Life Palace. If they differ a lot, you are often not yourself in the relationship. If they are close, the argument is probably about temperament rather than about the relationship." If children or housework are the topic, she adds the Children Palace (子女宮) and the Property Palace, because the stability of the home shapes family life. The wider habit of repeating a relationship pattern is covered in [Why You Keep Choosing the Wrong Partners in Zi Wei Dou Shu](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/why-you-keep-choosing-the-wrong-partners-in-zi-wei-dou-shu).

Workplace conflict has a similar structure. The Parents Palace stands for superiors, the Friends Palace (交友宮) for peers and subordinates, and the Career Palace for your own way of working. Finding which layer is troubled tells you whether to change how you speak to your manager or how you lead a team. Small actions work: write three bullet points before meeting the manager, or have lunch with one colleague each week.

Finally Ahmed mentions poor sleep. "Look at the Health Palace (疾厄宮) together with the Fortune Palace," says Lena. "When the Fortune Palace is uneasy and the Health Palace carries Hua Ji, it is often long-running worry affecting the body. It is a prompt for self-care and not a medical diagnosis. If sleep problems last, see a doctor." She also points to a marker on his chart. "That is the Body Palace. It is not a thirteenth palace but sits inside one of the twelve, and shows where your focus moves in the second half of life. The Life Palace is what you start with and the Body Palace is where you tend to end up." A practical view of sleep is in [Why Poor Sleep Is Not Weak Willpower in Zi Wei Dou Shu](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/why-poor-sleep-is-not-weak-willpower-in-zi-wei-dou-shu).

Lena leaves him a small assignment. Write the single question that bothers you most, find the three or four palaces that bear on it, and write one sentence about the role each plays. The anxiety usually separates into specific parts: some call for action, others only for acceptance. That is more memorable than reading all twelve palaces in sequence, and it protects you from being frightened by the name of one star.

Ahmed's notes at the end of the evening were short: look at the Life and Travel Palaces before moving, split money into earning, calm and assets, and compare the Spouse Palace with the Life Palace for relationship habits. To try this yourself, build a chart with the [chart tool](https://aiziwei.online/en/analysis), choose one question, and read only the palaces that bear on it.
