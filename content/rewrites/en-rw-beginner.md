Start with what this is. Zi Wei Dou Shu, the Chinese system of Purple Star Astrology, is a traditional symbolic system. It uses a person's birth time to place stars in twelve palaces and then reads those placements through a body of tradition. It is a cultural practice and a language of symbols, not a scientifically validated method, and nothing here predicts your future. The aim of this guide is to help you understand how a chart is organized. To show how a first reading goes, the guide follows two invented characters, Mina and her friend Joon. Neither is a real person.

## "I made a chart. Where do I start?"

Mina sends Joon a screenshot: twelve boxes, dozens of stars, abbreviations she cannot read. Joon's first question is not about stars. "Did you enter solar or lunar dates? Are you sure of the birth hour?" Mina says she used the solar calendar and her mother remembers "around noon." Joon asks her to build charts for the hours on either side of noon as well. The three charts place the Life Palace (命宮) differently. "So before you read anything, settle the hour. Otherwise you may be reading someone else's chart."

Mina asks her mother for the old birth record, and it confirms the noon hour. The lesson is that a difference of one day in the lunar date, or one hour in the birth time, rebuilds the whole chart. If the time is uncertain, compare neighboring hours and see whether the Life Palace moves.

## "What does this star mean, and is my bad palace a disaster?"

With the data settled, Mina points at the Life Palace. "Tian Ji and Tai Yin sit here. Is that good?" Joon says to set good and bad aside for a moment. "The Life Palace is your base temperament. Tian Ji (天機) is an analytical star, and Tai Yin (太陰) is observant and quiet. Together they often describe someone who thinks a lot and feels a lot." Mina laughs. She does tend to weigh decisions until her friends lose patience.

Joon recommends not trying to memorize all fourteen major stars. Learn the one or two in your own Life Palace first, and read about them later in separate guides such as [Detailed Explanation of the Tai Yin Star: Wisdom and Gentleness Under the Moonlight](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/detailed-explanation-of-the-tai-yin-star-wisdom-and-gentleness-under-the-moonlig).

A few days later Mina returns, worried. Her Career Palace has Qing Yang (擎羊), a harsh star, and a website says it means obstacles at work. Joon asks her to find three more palaces: the one directly opposite, and the two that form a triangle with the Career Palace. "We read in a group of four palaces. This is the Three Sides and Four Corners rule." Several supportive stars sit in those other palaces. "A harsh star in one palace is not necessarily harsh once the group is considered, and a lucky star can be pressed by an unlucky opposite. You cannot judge from one star." What each palace stands for is covered in [Complete Analysis of the Twelve Palaces in Zi Wei Dou Shu](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/complete-analysis-of-the-twelve-palaces-in-zi-wei-dou-shu-from-life-palace-to-fo).

## "What are the colored marks, and where am I now?"

On their third meeting Mina points to four marks labeled Lu, Quan, Ke and Ji. Joon explains that these are the four transformations: Hua Lu (化祿), Hua Quan (化權), Hua Ke (化科) and Hua Ji (化忌), set by the stem of the birth year. Mina was born in a Geng year, so Tai Yang gets Hua Lu, Wu Qu gets Hua Quan, Tai Yin gets Hua Ke and Tian Tong gets Hua Ji.

"Hua Ji is not bad luck," Joon says. "In our school it means fixation, the place where you find it hard to let go." Mina checks which palace Tian Tong with Hua Ji sits in and says, "No wonder I care so much about that area." Knowing your fixation is more helpful than fearing it. The system is described in more detail in [Four Transformations in Zi Wei Dou Shu: How Life Forces Shift](https://aiziwei.online/en/blog/four-transformations-in-zi-wei-dou-shu-how-life-forces-shift).

Mina then asks where she is in life. Joon helps her locate her current major cycle, a ten-year block that moves from palace to palace. At thirty-one she is in a cycle on the Career Palace, which points to work as the theme of the decade, matching her recent thoughts about leaving her job. That does not mean she must leave. It suggests that the next few years are a good time to decide on direction deliberately, so that whether she stays or goes it is a choice. Yearly changes belong on top of that, so the sequence is natal pattern first, then the ten-year cycle, then the year. Reversing it makes one year's turbulence look like a life event.

By the end Joon and Mina had a short list of habits. Confirm the data before reading. Learn the stars of the Life Palace first. Look at a group of four palaces before judging a harsh star. Ask one concrete question at a time and read only the palaces that relate to it. And if something is unclear, leave it for later rather than conclude from the name of a single star.
